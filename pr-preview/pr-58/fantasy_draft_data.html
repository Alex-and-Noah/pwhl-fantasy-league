Fantasy Draft Data

For the 2026-2027 season, we are making two changes to changes to the fantasy league regarding fantasy points and fantasy team drafting. First, the new structure for scoring fantasy points is as follows:

Second, all PWHL players will be assigned a Fantasy Draft Value (FDV), and a Value Cap will be imposed to the fantasy teams that will be drafted this year. The FDV will be computed based on the new fantasy point structure applied retroactively to the 2025-2026 season.

2026-2027 Season

Assigning the FDV

The FDV will be assigned differently based on whether the player is a new rookie in the upcoming season. In each case however, instead of looking at Fantasy Points (FP) earned last year, we look at the Projected Fantasy Points (PFP) earned last year. The PFP are defined as follows:

\[ \text{PFP} = 30 * \frac{\text{FP}}{\text{GP}}, \]

where \(GP:=\) games played.

We take the average number of FP earned per game played and multiply that by the total number of games in a season for one team (30) to estimate how many FP would have been earned if the player played every game of the season.

Non-rookie

If the player is not a rookie, the FDV is is simply the player’s earned PFP last season, rounded to the nearest integer.

Rookie

Note

We secretly replaced all PFPs less than \(\mathrm{e}\) with \(\mathrm{e}\), just so that the exponential fit would never yield negative values. You’ll see why soon…

Rather than using PFPs by overall draft position for the last season, we are going to use the exponential curve approximating the PFPs descending in order to inform how the FDVs get assigned to the rookies in the upcoming season. The formula for the FDV for new rookies will be as follows:

\[ \text{FDV} = \max\left( \text{round}\left(\exp \left( b + m\cdot\left(1+(\text{pos}_\text{draft} - 1)\cdot\frac{\text{N}_\text{rookies,t-1}-1}{\text{N}_\text{rookies,t}}\right)\right)\right), 1\right), \]

where:

  • \(\text{pos}_\text{draft}\in\{1,\ldots,\text{N}_\text{rookie,t}\}:=\) draft order position of new rookie
  • \(\text{N}_\text{rookies,t}:=\) number of new rookies in year \(t\) (this year)
  • \(\text{N}_\text{rookies,t-1}:=\) number of new rookies in year \(t-1\) (last year)
  • \(b:=\) intercept of the line fit to the \(\log\) of the descending PFPs (\(b=\) 3.7864868)
  • \(m:=\) slope of the line fit to the \(\log\) of the descending PFPs (\(m=\) -0.07382)

The idea behind this formula is that we map \(\text{N}_\text{rookies,t}\) equal points to the support of the fit exponential curve, which is \(\{1,\ldots,\text{N}_\text{rookie,t-1}\}\). Under this mapping, the first overall draft pick (K.K. Harvey for the 2026-2027 season) should be mapped to the PFP estimate corresponding to the first overall draft pick of the 2025-2026 season (Kristýna Kaltounková), the last overall draft pick for the 2026-2027 season should be mapped to the PFP estimate corresponding to the last overall draft pick of the 2025-2026 season, and each draft pick in between for the 2026-2027 season should be evenly spaced out between \(1\) and \(\text{N}_\text{rookie,t-1}\); this yields a spacing of \(\frac{\text{N}_\text{rookies,t-1}-1}{\text{N}_\text{rookies,t}}\)
